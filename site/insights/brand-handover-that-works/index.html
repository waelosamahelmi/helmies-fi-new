What belongs in a brand handover?

An identity is not finished when the presentation looks beautiful. It is ready when the people using it can make the next proposal, social post, or printed item without guessing how the brand works.

HELMIES JOURNAL · 5 OCTOBER 2026
IN THIS STORY

Build the handover around everyday tasks.

Ask what the team creates most often. A sales proposal, an email signature, a social campaign, and a product label each need different formats. Prioritize the tools that will be used next week rather than filling a folder with every possible variation.

Give each template a clear owner and a short explanation of what may change. A template becomes less useful when people cannot tell whether the headline size, image crop, or color is fixed or simply an example.

Make logo files understandable.

Organize logo variations by purpose: full identity, compact mark, light background, dark background, and single color. Include appropriate vector and raster formats within the agreed scope. Use filenames a colleague can understand without opening every file.

Show minimum-size and spacing examples in actual contexts. A rule explained through a small mobile header or a narrow label is easier to apply than an abstract diagram alone. Check the mark at the sizes where people will really encounter it.

Explain typography as a hierarchy.

A list of font names does not explain how to write a page. Show a headline, a subheading, body text, a caption, and a call to action together. Include examples with realistic lengths so the rules survive ordinary content.

Clarify font licensing and where the files come from. If a typeface is not available in a common office tool, define a suitable fallback and demonstrate it. This avoids a team improvising a different substitute in every document.

Give imagery a point of view.

Describe subjects, framing, lighting, color, and the relationship between images and text. Examples of what to avoid are useful when they explain the reason rather than simply marking a picture as wrong.

For generated or edited imagery, keep a consistent reference set and document the review process. Check factual details, unwanted text, and brand elements before release. A production method does not remove the need for art direction or permission to use source material.

Test the kit without the designer in the room.

Ask a colleague to create one realistic item using only the handover. Notice where they hesitate or invent a rule. Those moments reveal what the documentation needs to explain.

Finish with a contents page, a version date, and a place for the approved files. An identity will evolve, so separate current assets from old drafts. The best brand handover makes consistent work easier to produce, review, and maintain.
