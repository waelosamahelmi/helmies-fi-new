INTELLIGENCE / 2 MIN READ

Before an AI assistant, put your knowledge in order.

An assistant cannot resolve a company’s conflicting policies by sounding confident. Before connecting your documents, decide which information is current, who owns it, and who should be allowed to see it.

HELMIES JOURNAL · 5 OCTOBER 2026
IN THIS STORY

Start with a small trusted collection.

Choose the documents needed for one specific task. A focused support assistant might start with approved product guidance and a set of common questions. Connecting every shared folder at once makes it harder to understand where an answer came from.

Give each source an owner and a review date. Where two documents disagree, ask a person to resolve the conflict. Do not leave the assistant to choose which version is authoritative based on wording or file location.

Write for retrieval as well as reading.

Use descriptive headings and keep related information together. Define abbreviations and identify the product, policy, or process a section concerns. A paragraph copied out of a document should still contain enough context to be understood.

Separate instructions from examples. Make dates and exceptions explicit. If a policy applies only to one region or one customer group, include that condition near the rule rather than only in a cover page that may be separated during processing.

Map access before connecting systems.

Different people may have permission to read different sources. An assistant should not expose information simply because its integration account can see it. Design around the requesting user’s access and the sensitivity of the material.

Use representative but appropriate test data during early development. Avoid sending confidential records into a tool before the organization has reviewed the provider, settings, and intended use. The integration design should explain where information travels and who can inspect it.

Build questions with known answers.

Collect realistic questions and have a knowledgeable person write the expected answer and supporting source. Include questions with no answer in the collection, outdated information, and ambiguous wording.

Review whether the assistant cites the correct material, preserves exceptions, and admits when information is missing. A fluent answer is not enough. The evaluation should reflect the consequence of a mistake in the particular workflow.

Make maintenance part of the launch.

Choose how changes reach the assistant and how removed documents stop being used. Assign someone to review reported errors and update the trusted collection. A knowledge system is a living part of the business.

Start with a limited audience and gather feedback on actual tasks. Look for recurring missing information rather than treating every poor answer as a prompt problem. Sometimes the most useful improvement is a clearer source document that helps both people and the assistant.
