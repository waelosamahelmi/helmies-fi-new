INTELLIGENCE / 2 MIN READ

Useful AI begins with a smaller question.

“Add AI” is not yet a product brief. “Help our team find the right policy and show where it came from” is a much more useful beginning.

HELMIES JOURNAL · 5 OCTOBER 2026
IN THIS STORY
Choose the job to be done.Know the information behind the answer.Make uncertainty visible.Test with the awkward examples.Measure usefulness, not novelty.

Choose the job to be done.

Start with a real moment in someone’s work. What are they trying to understand, produce, or decide? Describe the current difficulty and the result that would help. This gives the team something concrete to design and evaluate.

Know the information behind the answer.

An assistant depends on the material it can access. Review relevance, freshness, permissions, and missing context. The quality of a response cannot be separated from the quality of the information and instructions behind it.

Make uncertainty visible.

An interface should not make every response look equally certain. Where appropriate, provide source references, explain limits, and offer a route to a person. Design the experience around the possibility of an incomplete or incorrect answer.

Test with the awkward examples.

Use realistic questions, ambiguous requests, unavailable information, and cases where the assistant should decline a task. A demonstration of the easiest case says little about how the system will behave in everyday work.

Measure usefulness, not novelty.

Look at whether users complete the task more effectively, whether the output needs correction, and whether the workflow fits their habits. Sometimes the best result is a small assistant that does one job clearly and predictably.
